# Public Assessment Report — Paracetamol pxgpharma 500 mg Film-Coated Tablets

**Marketing authorisation holder:** PXG Pharma GmbH (Germany)

**Document date:** 8 December 2023
**Retrieved:** 17 August 2026

## I. Introduction
Marketing authorisation granted for Paracetamol pxgpharma 500 mg film-coated tablets (PXG Pharma GmbH, Germany). Indicated for short-term symptomatic treatment of mild to moderate pain and/or fever, in adults, adolescents, and children with body weight from 22 kg (over 6 years of age).

Granted under Article 10(1) of Directive 2001/83/EC (generic application). Essential similarity was demonstrated against the innovator product **Panodil 500 mg tablets** (registered in Denmark by GlaxoSmithKline Consumer Healthcare A/S since 14 August 1974).

Concerned Member States in this decentralised procedure: Czechia, Estonia, Hungary, Lithuania, Latvia, Romania, Slovakia.

## II. Quality aspects
- Film-coated, caplet-shaped tablet, debossed "PARA500," with a score line.
- Each tablet: 500 mg paracetamol.
- Tablet core excipients: pregelatinized maize starch, povidone K-30, sodium maize starch glycolate (type A), stearic acid (E570).
- Film-coating: hypromellose (E464), macrogol 400 (E1521).
- Pack sizes: 10 (blister), 20/30/50 (blister), 24/100/300/500/1000 (HDPE bottle).
- Active substance: established Ph.Eur.-monographed paracetamol, white/almost-white crystalline powder; sparingly soluble in water, freely soluble in ethanol (96%).
- Manufactured via wet granulation, drying, blending, lubrication, compression, coating — conventional techniques, validated per EU guidelines.
- Drug substance stable 5 years under stated conditions (per CEP/EDQM assessment).
- Finished product: shelf life 36 months, no special storage condition required. Stable under light exposure (photostability tested). No TSE (transmissible spongiform encephalopathy) risk — no ruminant-derived substances used.
- A nitrosamine impurity risk evaluation was submitted and found acceptable, per EMA/369136/2020 and ICH Q3D elemental impurity requirements.

## III. Non-clinical aspects
- No new non-clinical (pharmacology/pharmacokinetics/toxicology) studies were required — justified by reference to existing literature on paracetamol, a well-established active substance.
- Environmental risk assessment not required, since this generic does not increase overall environmental exposure to paracetamol.

## IV. Clinical aspects
- No new clinical studies were conducted for this generic application — reference made to the well-established efficacy/safety profile of paracetamol and clinical experience with the innovator product (Panodil).
- **BCS (Biopharmaceutics Classification System) Class I biowaiver** granted instead of requiring in vivo bioequivalence studies. Requirements met:
  - High solubility and complete absorption of paracetamol as a drug substance.
  - Very rapid dissolution demonstrated (≥85% dissolved within 15 minutes, tested in water and at 4 different pH buffers: 0.1N HCl pH 1.2, acetate buffer pH 4.5, phosphate buffer pH 5.8, and pH 6.8), matching the reference product Panodil.
  - No excipients present that could affect bioavailability.
- **Risk Management Plan (RMP):** no important identified risks, no important potential risks, no missing information flagged. Member states agreed routine pharmacovigilance and routine risk minimisation measures are sufficient.

## V. User consultation
The package leaflet was tested via a user consultation study (Articles 59(3) and 61(1) of Directive 2001/83/EC): a pilot with 2 participants, followed by two rounds of 10 participants each, assessing traceability, comprehensibility, and applicability. Results met the EU readability guideline criteria for patient information leaflets.

## VI. Overall conclusion and recommendation
- Chemical-pharmaceutical quality proven.
- Bioequivalence to Panodil demonstrated per EU guidance.
- No discussion required at CMD(h) level — agreement reached via written procedure among member states.
- Decentralised procedure finalised with a **positive outcome** on 19 July 2023.

## Why this document is useful alongside the SmPCs
This PAR does not restate dosing/contraindication information already covered in the product's SmPC — it explains *why regulators were satisfied* the generic could be approved without new clinical trials (via BCS Class I biowaiver and dissolution-profile comparison to the reference product), and confirms no additional safety concerns were identified during review. It's a good source for "why was this approved" or "how was bioequivalence established" style questions, which the SmPC alone doesn't answer.
