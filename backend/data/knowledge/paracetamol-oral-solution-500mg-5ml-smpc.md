# Paracetamol 500 mg/5 ml Oral Solution — Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC)

**Source:** electronic Medicines Compendium (emc), UK — https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/3233/smpc
**Marketing authorisation holder:** Mercury Pharmaceuticals Ltd / ADVANZ Pharma
**Marketing authorisation number:** PL 12762/0173
**Document last updated (per source):** 7 February 2025
**Retrieved:** 17 August 2026
**Document type:** Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) — for use by healthcare professionals

---

## 1. Name of the medicinal product
Paracetamol 500 mg/5 ml Oral Solution (also marketed as Vetopar 500mg/5ml Oral Solution)

## 2. Composition
Each 5 ml contains 500 mg paracetamol.
Excipients with known effect: methyl parahydroxybenzoate (E218) 2 mg/5ml, propyl parahydroxybenzoate (E216) 0.5 mg/5ml, glycerol (E422) 1500 mg/5ml, propylene glycol (E1520) 1500 mg/5ml, sodium citrate 7.5 mg/5ml, saccharin sodium 20 mg/5ml.

## 3. Pharmaceutical form
Clear, pink, viscous solution with raspberry odour.

## 4. Clinical particulars

### 4.1 Therapeutic indications
Management of pain and fever (e.g. common cold, influenza, headache). Intended for patients unable to tolerate solid-dose formulations or lower-strength paracetamol preparations.

### 4.2 Posology and method of administration
**Adults and young persons 16+:** 500 mg (5 ml) to 1000 mg (10 ml), three to four times daily as required, up to a maximum of 4 g/day (40 ml). Not more frequently than every 4 hours; no more than 4 doses in 24 hours.

**Elderly:** Absorption rate/extent normal, but plasma half-life longer and clearance lower than in young adults.

**Paediatric population:** Do NOT use in children and adolescents under 16 years (note: this differs from the tablet formulation, which permits use from age 10).

Administration: oral only; shake bottle for at least 10 seconds before use.

### 4.3 Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to paracetamol or any excipient.
- Severe hepatic dysfunction.
- Children and adolescents under 16 years.

### 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use
- Caution in severe renal or severe hepatic impairment; greater overdose hazard in non-cirrhotic alcoholic liver disease.
- Do not combine with other paracetamol-containing products.
- HAGMA (high anion gap metabolic acidosis) due to pyroglutamic acidosis risk in severely ill, malnourished, or chronic-alcoholism patients on prolonged therapeutic dosing, or on paracetamol + flucloxacillin — same profile as the tablet formulation (see paracetamol-tablets-500mg-smpc.md).
- **Excipient-specific warnings (not present in the tablet formulation):**
  - Parahydroxybenzoates may cause allergic reactions (possibly delayed).
  - Contains 1530 mg propylene glycol per 5ml — use with caution in pregnancy, breastfeeding, liver or kidney disease.
  - Glycerol may cause headache, stomach upset, diarrhoea.
  - Contains 4.25 mg/5ml (0.18 mmol) sodium — considered "sodium free."

### 4.5 Interactions with other medicinal products
- Hepatotoxicity (particularly after overdose) may be increased by drugs inducing liver microsomal enzymes: barbiturates, tricyclic antidepressants, alcohol.
- Absorption speed increased by metoclopramide/domperidone; reduced by cholestyramine (as with tablets).
- Prolonged regular use may enhance warfarin/coumarin anticoagulant effect, increasing bleeding risk.
- **Zidovudine:** regular paracetamol use possibly reduces zidovudine metabolism, increasing neutropenia risk — this interaction is listed in the oral solution SmPC but not explicitly in the tablet SmPC.
- Concomitant flucloxacillin: HAGMA risk (see 4.4).

### 4.6 Fertility, pregnancy and lactation
**Pregnancy:** Same profile as tablets — no ill effects shown at recommended dosage in epidemiological studies; no malformative or feto/neonatal toxicity from large datasets; neurodevelopmental data inconclusive. Use at lowest effective dose, shortest duration, lowest frequency if clinically needed.

**Breastfeeding:** Excreted in breast milk but not in clinically significant amounts; does not contraindicate breastfeeding.

**Fertility:** No data on human fertility effects.

### 4.7 Effects on driving and using machines
None known.

### 4.8 Undesirable effects
| System Organ Class | Frequency | Adverse Events |
|---|---|---|
| Blood and lymphatic | Not known | Blood dyscrasia incl. thrombocytopenia, agranulocytosis |
| Immune system | Not known | Anaphylactic shock, angioedema, anaphylactic reaction, urticaria, hypersensitivity, rash |
| Metabolism/nutrition | Not known | High anion gap metabolic acidosis |
| Gastrointestinal | Not known | Acute pancreatitis |
| Skin/subcutaneous | Very rare | Serious skin reactions |
| Renal/urinary | Uncommon | Toxic nephropathy |

Report suspected adverse reactions via the MHRA Yellow Card Scheme.

### 4.9 Overdose
Same thresholds as tablet formulation: liver damage possible at ≥10 g in adults; ≥5 g if risk factors present (same risk-factor list: enzyme-inducing drugs, excess alcohol, glutathione depletion states).

**Symptoms:** as per tablets, plus hyperglycaemia has been reported for this formulation.

**Management:** identical protocol to tablets — urgent hospital referral, activated charcoal within 1 hour, plasma level at 4+ hours, N-acetylcysteine (max protective effect within 8h, usable to 24h), oral methionine as a remote-area alternative, specialist input for late-presenting hepatic dysfunction.

## 5. Pharmacological properties

### 5.1 Pharmacodynamics
ATC code N02BE01. Mechanism: reduces fever via direct action on hypothalamic heat-regulating centres (vasodilation, sweating); inhibits endogenous pyrogen action. Nearly as potent as aspirin at inhibiting CNS prostaglandin synthetase, but minimal peripheral inhibition — explains lack of significant anti-inflammatory/anti-rheumatic effect. Does not inhibit platelet aggregation, affect prothrombin response, or cause GI ulceration.

### 5.2 Pharmacokinetics
Rapid, near-complete GI absorption; peak plasma concentration 0.5–2 hours (liquid formulation absorbs slightly faster than tablets). Usual analgesic doses: serum concentration 5–20 mcg/ml. Serum protein binding 20–50% at toxic concentrations. ~4% metabolised via cytochrome P-450 to a toxic metabolite, normally detoxified via hepatic glutathione conjugation. Chronic use or large acute doses deplete glutathione stores, risking hepatic necrosis. Elimination half-life 1–4 hours (slightly longer in neonates: 2.2–5 hours, and in cirrhotic patients).

## 6. Pharmaceutical particulars
Shelf life: 24 months unopened; 3 months after opening. Store below 25°C; do not refrigerate or freeze.

## 9–10. Authorisation
First authorised 13 February 2007 (renewed 13 October 2011). Text last revised 7 February 2025.

## Key differences vs. the 500 mg tablet formulation
- **Paediatric age cutoff differs:** oral solution is contraindicated under 16 years; tablets permit use from age 10.
- **Severe hepatic dysfunction** is an explicit contraindication for the oral solution but only a "caution" (not a contraindication) for tablets.
- Oral solution carries additional excipient-specific warnings (propylene glycol, parahydroxybenzoates, glycerol) not present in the tablet formulation.
- Zidovudine interaction is explicitly listed for the oral solution but not the tablet SmPC.
