## 1. Name of the medicinal product
Paracetamol 500 mg Tablets

## 2. Composition
Each tablet contains 500 mg of paracetamol.
Excipients: maize starch, purified talc, stearic acid, povidone, soluble starch.

## 3. Pharmaceutical form
White capsule-shaped tablet with flat edges.

## 4. Clinical particulars

### 4.1 Therapeutic indications
Mild analgesic and antipyretic. Indicated for most painful and febrile conditions, e.g. headache including migraine, toothache, neuralgia, colds and influenza, sore throat, backache, rheumatic pain, and dysmenorrhoea.

### 4.2 Posology and method of administration
**Adults, elderly, and children over 16 years:** Two tablets every four hours as required. Maximum eight tablets in 24 hours. Doses should not be given more frequently than every four hours, and no more than four doses in any 24-hour period. Do not take for more than 3 days without consulting a doctor.

**Paediatric population:** Not recommended for children under 10 years.
- Children 10–15 years: one tablet every four to six hours as needed, maximum four doses in 24 hours. Not for more than 3 days without medical advice.

Route: oral.

### 4.3 Contraindications
Hypersensitivity to paracetamol or to any excipient listed above.

### 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use
- Not recommended for children under 10 years.
- Caution in patients with severe renal or severe hepatic impairment — overdose hazard is greater in patients with non-cirrhotic alcoholic liver disease.
- Do not exceed the recommended dose; do not take with other paracetamol-containing products.
- Cases of high anion gap metabolic acidosis (HAGMA) due to pyroglutamic acidosis have been reported in severely ill patients (e.g. severe renal impairment, sepsis, malnutrition, chronic alcoholism) on prolonged therapeutic-dose paracetamol, or on a paracetamol + flucloxacillin combination. If suspected, discontinue paracetamol promptly and monitor closely; urinary 5-oxoproline measurement may help identify the cause.
- Seek immediate medical advice in the event of overdose, even if the patient feels well, due to risk of delayed serious liver damage.

### 4.5 Interactions with other medicinal products
- Absorption speed increased by metoclopramide or domperidone; reduced by cholestyramine.
- Prolonged regular use may enhance the anticoagulant effect of warfarin and other coumarins, increasing bleeding risk. Occasional doses have no significant effect.
- Concomitant use with flucloxacillin has been associated with high anion gap metabolic acidosis (pyroglutamic acidosis), particularly in patients with risk factors (see 4.4).

### 4.6 Fertility, pregnancy and lactation
**Pregnancy:** Epidemiological studies show no ill effects at recommended dosage; a large body of data indicates no malformative or feto/neonatal toxicity. Neurodevelopmental studies in children exposed in utero are inconclusive. If clinically needed, use at the lowest effective dose, for the shortest time, at the lowest possible frequency.

**Breastfeeding:** Paracetamol is excreted in breast milk but not in clinically significant amounts; available data do not contraindicate breastfeeding.

### 4.7 Effects on driving and using machines
No influence on ability to drive or use machines.

### 4.8 Undesirable effects
- Immune system: hypersensitivity/skin rash may occur; not known frequency — anaphylactic shock, angioedema.
- Blood/lymphatic: not known — blood dyscrasias including thrombocytopenia and agranulocytosis.
- Skin: very rare serious skin reactions.
- Metabolism/nutrition: not known — high anion gap metabolic acidosis (see 4.4), linked to pyroglutamic acidosis from low glutathione levels in at-risk patients.
- Suspected adverse reactions should be reported to the MHRA Yellow Card Scheme.

### 4.9 Overdose
Liver damage possible in adults after ingestion of ≥10 g paracetamol. In patients with risk factors, ≥5 g may cause liver damage.

**Risk factors:** long-term treatment with liver-enzyme-inducing drugs (carbamazepine, phenobarbitone, phenytoin, primidone, rifampicin, St John's Wort, etc.); regular ethanol consumption above recommended limits; likely glutathione depletion (eating disorders, cystic fibrosis, HIV infection, starvation, cachexia).

**Symptoms (first 24h):** pallor, nausea, vomiting, anorexia, abdominal pain. Liver damage may become apparent 12–48 hours after ingestion. Possible abnormalities in glucose metabolism and metabolic acidosis. Severe poisoning may progress to hepatic failure, encephalopathy, disseminated intravascular coagulation, haemorrhage, hypoglycaemia, cerebral oedema, GI bleeding, and death. Acute renal failure (acute tubular necrosis) may occur even without severe liver damage — suggested by loin pain, haematuria, proteinuria. Cardiac arrhythmias and pancreatitis have been reported.

**Management:** Urgent hospital referral even with minimal early symptoms. Activated charcoal if within 1 hour of overdose. Plasma paracetamol concentration measured at 4+ hours post-ingestion (earlier levels unreliable). N-acetylcysteine (NAC) can be used up to 24 hours post-ingestion, though maximum protective effect is within 8 hours; effectiveness declines sharply after that. IV NAC per established dosage schedule; oral methionine may be an alternative outside hospital if vomiting isn't an issue. Serious hepatic dysfunction presenting beyond 24h should be discussed with a poisons information service or liver unit.

## 5. Pharmacological properties

### 5.1 Pharmacodynamics
ATC code N02BE01. Effective analgesic and antipyretic with only weak anti-inflammatory action. Mechanism not fully understood — likely central inhibition of prostaglandin synthesis in the CNS, plus a peripheral component. Antipyretic action likely via hypothalamic heat-regulating centre (vasodilation, sweating, heat loss). No cardiovascular/respiratory effects; unlike salicylates, does not cause gastric irritation or bleeding.

### 5.2 Pharmacokinetics
Readily absorbed orally; peak plasma concentration 30 min–2 hours post-ingestion. Metabolised in the liver (90–95%); excreted in urine mainly as glucuronide/sulphate conjugates (<5% unchanged). Elimination half-life approx. 1–4 hours. Plasma protein binding negligible at therapeutic concentrations. A minor toxic metabolite (NAPQI) is normally detoxified via glutathione conjugation but can accumulate and cause liver damage after overdose.

## 6. Pharmaceutical particulars
Shelf life: 36 months. Store in original package. Various pack sizes (polypropylene containers, blister strips, HDPE containers).
