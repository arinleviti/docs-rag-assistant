# What Is a Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC)?

**Source:** European Medicines Agency (EMA) — official explanatory presentation — https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/presentation/presentation-summary-product-characteristics_en.pdf
**Author (per source):** Olayinka Fasanya, Medical Information — Information Compliance and Consistency, EMA
**Retrieved:** 17 August 2026
**Document type:** EMA reference/explainer — describes what an SmPC is, where to find one, and how it's structured. This is a meta-document: it does not contain product-specific information, but explains how to interpret documents like the two paracetamol SmPCs in this knowledge base.

---

## 1. What is the SmPC?
The SmPC is a **legal document** approved as part of a medicine's marketing authorisation. It is the primary basis of information for healthcare professionals on how to use a medicine. Its content is updated throughout the product's life-cycle as new data emerge.

## 2. Where SmPC information can be found
- Competent authorities' websites: the European Medicines Agency and National Competent Authorities.
- Medicines compendia/dictionaries (e.g. the UK's electronic Medicines Compendium, emc).
- SmPCs are the primary source for medical/pharmaceutical references and electronic prescribing support tools.
- All advertising must comply with the SmPC.
- Patient package leaflets are derived from SmPC information.

## 3. What information is in the SmPC
- Essential information for use of the medicine.
- Qualitative and quantitative information on benefits and risks.
- Information for individualised care: paediatric/elderly populations, organ impairment, concomitant disease, drug interactions, genomic factors, pregnancy/lactation/fertility, composition (hypersensitivity prevention, excipients with known effects), specific situations.
- Pharmaceutical information.

## 4. Structure of the SmPC
Information follows a predefined, standardised structure across all SmPCs. Some information could logically sit in multiple sections; cross-references are used instead of repeating content.

### Standard section map (as used across EU/UK SmPCs, including the paracetamol documents in this knowledge base):
- **4.1 Therapeutic indications** — target disease/condition and population.
- **4.2 Posology and method of administration** — dose per indication/subpopulation, frequency, food interaction, treatment duration, dose adjustment guidance, max dose, missed-dose guidance.
- **4.3 Contraindications** — patient populations who must NOT take the medicine for safety reasons.
- **4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use** — risks requiring precaution/monitoring, at-risk patient groups, risks healthcare professionals must be alerted to.
- **4.5 Interaction with other medicinal products** — drug-drug and drug-food interactions; related posology/warning/contraindication cross-references appear in 4.2/4.4/4.3 respectively.
- **4.6 Fertility, pregnancy and lactation** — use during pregnancy, breastfeeding recommendations, contraception needs, fertility effects.
- **4.7 Effects on ability to drive and use machines.**
- **4.8 Undesirable effects** — safety profile summary, tabulated adverse reactions by system organ class and frequency, special population differences.
- **4.9 Overdose** — symptoms and management.
- **5.1 Pharmacodynamic properties** — mechanism of action, key clinical trial results supporting authorisation, population studied, special-population data (including paediatric data even if not yet indicated for children).
- **5.2 Pharmacokinetic properties** — absorption, distribution, metabolism, elimination.
- **6.1–6.6 Pharmaceutical particulars** — excipients (6.1), incompatibilities (6.2), shelf life (6.3), storage precautions (6.4), container/pack details (6.5), disposal/handling (6.6).

## 5–8. Individualised care information
- **Paediatric/elderly populations:** mandatory information appears across multiple sections (4.2, 4.4, 4.5, 5.1, etc.) when a clinically relevant difference exists for these groups.
- **Hepatic/renal impairment:** dose adjustment guidance in 4.2; pharmacokinetic profile differences in 5.2.
- **Concomitant diseases:** special warnings in 4.4 (subheadings); dose adjustment in 4.2; contraindications in 4.3.
- **Drug/food interactions (4.5):** posology adjustment, precautions, or contraindications arising from an interaction are cross-referenced into 4.2, 4.4, or 4.3 respectively.
- **Genomic factors (pharmacogenomics):** included when clinically relevant — may affect indication, posology, dose adjustment, contraindications, or safety information.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, fertility (4.6):** use-during-pregnancy recommendation, breastfeeding continue/stop guidance, contraception needs with rationale, fertility effect data if available.
- **Excipients/hypersensitivity:** full composition listed in 6.1; excipients with known effects highlighted in sections 2 and 4.4 with a description of the risk.

## 9. Pharmaceutical information
Incompatibilities (6.2), shelf life including post-reconstitution shelf life where relevant (6.3), storage/disposal/handling precautions (6.4, 6.6).

## 10. How SmPC information is prepared
Pharmaceutical industry develops the medicine (drug discovery → preclinical trials → clinical trials, per international guidelines) and submits a dossier with a proposed SmPC to the relevant Medicine Competent Authority. The authority assesses quality, safety, and efficacy, evaluates how the SmPC will optimise benefits and manage risks, and approves the SmPC. **Post-marketing:** new clinical trials, adverse reaction reports, and epidemiological data collected after approval are assessed by the competent authority, and the SmPC is updated accordingly — it is a living document.

## 11. What is NOT included in the SmPC
- Detailed scientific development information — this is available instead in the **Public Assessment Report (PAR)**.
- Non-approved indications (not claimed by the marketing authorisation holder, or claimed but not supported by a positive benefit-risk demonstration) — except that paediatric-relevant trial data must be included per the EU Paediatric Regulation even if the product isn't yet indicated for children.
- Specific issues where data is lacking.
- General advice on treating particular medical conditions (the SmPC is product-specific, not a treatment guideline).

## 12–13. Maintaining information quality / further resources
The SmPC is updated as new adverse reactions or safety communications emerge; healthcare professionals and patients are encouraged to report suspected adverse reactions under EU pharmacovigilance legislation. Further information: European Medicines Agency (ema.europa.eu), EudraSmPC, and the EMA's "Ask EMA" service.
